# Transfer check: When a tie moves

Complete the first part without a chatbot. The transfer data are fictional and refer to a different library pair. The period definitions match across sites; the counts are visits, not distinct people.

| Site | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Hill | 40 | 45 |
| Park | 80 | 90 |

A comparison note says, “Hill and Park increased equally, so either site should be chosen for expansion.” Write a row for each interpretation of “equally” and then a separate row about expansion. Compute both changes, cite each cell, and identify a confound that the table cannot address. Decide what step the evidence supports.

## Review only after saving your answer

Hill gained 5 visits and Park gained 10. Both rose 12.5% relative to their own baseline: 5/40 and 10/80. The absolute tie has disappeared; the relative tie is new. Neither describes what an intervention caused. A past change is not enough to choose an expansion site. If a claim rests on distinct readers or comparable opportunities to visit, these totals do not supply that information.

Score the unaided answer one point each: units and windows correct; four named cells; both correct count changes; both correct relative changes and denominators; separate unsupported expansion decision; specific evidence request.
